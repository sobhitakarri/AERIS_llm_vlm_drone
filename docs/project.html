Zero-shot language-guided micro-quadrotor flight & multi-gate visual reasoning. AERIS translates natural language instructions into safe, depth-verified 3D trajectories and semantic maneuvers without task-specific training. Validated across 16 language-grounded target hover missions and 6 visual-reasoning multi-gate traversals on a 45g LiteWing nano-quadrotor and a high-fidelity Simscape Multibody digital twin.

Abstract

Autonomous multirotor flight from natural-language instructions requires bridging the gap between high-latency, non-deterministic foundation models (~0.1 Hz) and fast, safety-critical attitude stabilization (500–1000 Hz). Furthermore, existing vision-language models (VLMs) frequently hallucinate 2D bounding boxes on empty floors, causing naive robotic systems to crash. In this paper, we present AERIS, a modular closed-loop aerial navigation framework that decouples task planning, spatial grounding, and motor control into three strictly isolated tiers.

To eliminate hallucination risks, AERIS introduces depth-verified metric grounding: the overhead depth stream is back-projected into an obstacle height map to segment true physical objects, and VLM bounding boxes are rejected if they do not intersect a real physical mass. The validated goal is translated into atomic drone skills (TAKEOFF, MOVE_TO, HOVER, ROTATE, INSPECT, LAND) and monitored by a deterministic safety shield that clamps altitude and velocity.

To validate the entire pipeline prior to hazardous flight, we co-design a high-fidelity digital twin in MATLAB/Simulink and Simscape Multibody featuring contact dynamics, actuator saturation, non-linear battery discharge, sensor noise, and multi-camera rendering. Evaluated on 16 diverse language missions in an indoor laboratory scene, AERIS achieves 100% mission success, a best-case target hover error of 0.8 cm, an overhead camera calibration reprojection error of 0.78 px, and successful zero-shot dynamic replanning when a target is displaced by 36 cm mid-hover. All 302 software unit tests pass. Finally, the identical stack is mapped to an open-hardware 45-gram LiteWing ESP32-S3 nano-quadrotor over Wi-Fi, establishing a reproducible, accessible benchmark for foundation-model robotics in education and research.

Method Overview

We formulate language-guided aerial navigation as a hierarchical closed-loop control problem. At each mission epoch, the system processes a free-form natural language instruction $\ell$ along with calibrated multi-camera observations $I_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ and depth measurements $D_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ to generate an executable flight plan.

The architecture operates across three decoupled layers running at distinct frequencies: (1) Task Layer (~0.1–0.2 Hz): extracts intent and visual queries, leveraging a frozen Vision-Language Model to identify target candidates and generate atomic skill sequences; (2) Mission Layer (5–10 Hz): back-projects metric depth to segment physical objects, verifies 2D VLM boxes against physical geometry, validates safety constraints, and generates obstacle-aware trajectories; (3) Flight Layer (500–1000 Hz): executes cascaded PIV loops on high-frequency state estimates to drive the physical motors deterministically.

Formal safety shields enforce altitude and velocity bounds before commands reach the flight layer over UDP.

1. VLM-Based Semantic Intent & Skill Sequence Planning

We frame the task layer as an open-vocabulary semantic parsing problem. When an instruction $\ell$ arrives at the Intent Router, it is classified into one of three execution categories:

$$C_{\text{intent}} = \operatorname{classify}(\ell) \in \{\text{GEOMETRIC}, \text{VISUAL\_SEARCH}, \text{INSPECTION}\}$$

Geometric instructions (e.g. "fly 0.5m and make a square" in Case 15, or "fly in a star pattern" in Case 17) short-circuit the foundation model entirely, producing deterministic trajectories with zero model latency ($0.1\text{ s}$). For object-referencing commands, the sentence is routed to a frozen Vision-Language Model $G$ (Gemini 2.5 Flash API or local Qwen2-VL-2B) which outputs a normalized 2D bounding box:

$$B_{\text{2D}} = G(I_t, \ell) = [y_{\min}, x_{\min}, y_{\max}, x_{\max}] \in [0, 1000]^4$$

The target is then mapped into a sequence of atomic skill primitives $\mathcal{S} = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_k\}$, where $s_i \in \{\texttt{TAKEOFF}, \texttt{MOVE\_TO}, \texttt{HOVER}, \texttt{ROTATE}, \texttt{CIRCLE}, \texttt{INSPECT}, \texttt{LAND}\}$. Low-level motor control never touches the foundation model.

2. Depth-Verified Physical Object Proposals & 3D Grounding

A critical failure mode of standard 2D VLMs in robotics is floor hallucination: when prompted with an absent object, models routinely predict bounding boxes over empty linoleum floor. In existing ungrounded drone systems, this results in immediate collision or flight to empty space.

AERIS resolves this by decoupling semantic recognition from physical existence. The overhead depth map $D_t$ is back-projected into a 3D metric point cloud using the calibrated intrinsic matrix $K$ and extrinsic pose $[R_{cw} \mid t_{wc}]$ derived from six surveyed ground ArUco markers ($0.78\text{ px}$ reprojection error):

$$\begin{pmatrix} X_c \\ Y_c \\ Z_c \end{pmatrix} = d(u, v) \cdot K^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad P_w = R_{cw}^{\top} \begin{pmatrix} X_c \\ Y_c \\ Z_c \end{pmatrix} + t_{wc}$$

The ground plane is identified and removed via RANSAC. The remaining points are converted into an occupancy height map and clustered into physical 3D object proposals $\{O_1, O_2, \dots, O_m\}$, each defined by a 3D centroid, bounding sphere, and height. The 2D VLM bounding box $B_{\text{2D}}$ is then projected onto the proposal set:

$$\operatorname{Match}(B_{\text{2D}}, \{O_j\}) = \begin{cases} P_w(O_k) & \text{if } \exists k \text{ s.t. } \operatorname{IoU}(B_{\text{2D}}, \operatorname{Proj}(O_k)) > \tau_{\text{IoU}} \\ \text{REFUSE} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

If no physical mass exists beneath the VLM bounding box, the framework safely aborts (e.g. Case 08: "Find the purple elephant" is refused, resulting in zero flight). When a valid match is confirmed, the true metric coordinates are passed to the trajectory planner.

3. Deterministic Safety Shield & Cascaded Flight Control

Before any skill reaches execution, it must pass a deterministic safety shield that cannot be overridden by prompt injections or model hallucinations:

  • Altitude Clamping: Commanded heights are strictly clamped to $z \le 0.30\text{ m}$ on hardware ($1.50\text{ m}$ on twin).
  • Velocity & Geofencing: Velocity is capped at $0.40\text{ m/s}$, and flight is bounded within $\pm 1.0\text{ m}$ of takeoff.
  • Hardware Invariants: Loss of optical flow (0.3s), tilt $>50^\circ$, or battery voltage $<3.0\text{ V}$ triggers an immediate controlled descent.

The Flight Layer executes a 4-tier cascaded architecture: Position P loop (100 Hz) $\to$ Velocity PI loop with anti-windup (250 Hz) $\to$ Attitude P loop $\to$ Angular Rate PID loop (500–1000 Hz) $\to$ PWM motor mixer.

4. Closed-Loop Target Tracking & Dynamic Replanning

Unlike open-loop scripted waypoints, AERIS continuously tracks target centroids in the overhead height map at 10 Hz during hover or standoff inspection. If an external perturbation shifts the target position by $\Delta p > 15\text{ cm}$:

$$\Delta p(t) = \|p_{\text{target}}(t) - p_{\text{target}}(0)\| > 0.15\text{ m} \implies \operatorname{TriggerReplan}()$$

The active trajectory is interrupted, an updated smooth path is interpolated within 0.4 seconds, and the quadrotor converges to the relocated goal (evaluated in Case 09, where a 36 cm mid-hover target shift is successfully tracked with 5.0 cm final accuracy).

5. Language-Guided Multi-Gate Traversal via Onboard Camera Geometry

Beyond overhead floor-plane navigation, AERIS supports egocentric multi-gate traversal (e.g. "fly through the gate with the dog", "move towards the gate with the number equal to 2+4"). In this mode, the onboard forward-facing camera ($I_{\text{fpv}}$) captures a row of gates, where each gate comprises a framed picture on top, an identifying geometric shape, and an opening beneath ($Z_{\text{open}} = 0.525\text{ m}$).

A single VLM query examines the candidate gates from left to right, executes semantic or arithmetic reasoning over their pictures (e.g., evaluating $2+4=6$, distinguishing healthy fruit salad from fast food, or recognizing Tux the Linux penguin), and returns a tight 2D bounding box $B_{\text{gate}}$ around the target picture. The 3D metric coordinate of the gate is then triangulated purely from pinhole camera geometry:

$$t_{\text{width}} = \frac{f_x \cdot W_{\text{spec}}}{(u_1 - u_0) \cdot (\hat{\bm{r}} \cdot \hat{\bm{a}})}, \quad t_{\text{height}} = \frac{Z_{\text{center}} - Z_{\text{origin}}}{\hat{\bm{r}}_z}, \quad t = 0.25 \, t_{\text{width}} + 0.75 \, t_{\text{height}}$$

where $W_{\text{spec}} = 0.50\text{ m}$ is the standardized picture width, $\hat{\bm{r}}$ is the viewing ray vector, and $\hat{\bm{a}}$ is the optical axis. The planner synthesizes a collision-free traversal sequence:

$$\bm{p}_{\text{approach}} = \bm{p}_{\text{gate}} - \bm{d} \cdot 0.75\text{m} \;\longrightarrow\; \bm{p}_{\text{open}} = \bm{p}_{\text{gate}} (z=0.525\text{m}) \;\longrightarrow\; \bm{p}_{\text{exit}} = \bm{p}_{\text{gate}} + \bm{d} \cdot 0.85\text{m}$$

This achieves smooth, high-precision flight through the gate opening without needing ground-truth gate coordinates.

Hardware & Digital Twin Co-Design

To make language-guided drone research accessible without costly motion capture setups, AERIS is engineered for dual execution: an ultra-lightweight open-hardware nano-quadrotor and a physics-faithful digital twin.

LiteWing ESP32-S3 Nano-Quadrotor (Circuit Digest, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)

Target A: LiteWing ESP32-S3 Micro-Quadrotor

  • Mass & Frame: 45 g without battery; 100 mm × 100 mm FR4 integrated PCB airframe.
  • Computation & Comms: ESP32-S3 dual-core Xtensa LX7 @ 240 MHz, Wi-Fi UDP socket interface.
  • Sensors: InvenSense MPU-6050 6-DOF IMU, PMW3901 downward optical flow, VL53L1X laser rangefinder.
  • Actuation: Four 720 coreless brushed motors with 55 mm propellers driven by IRLML6344 MOSFETs.

Target B: Simscape Multibody Digital Twin

  • Multibody Dynamics: 6-DOF non-linear rigid body dynamics with floor/wall spring-damper contact models.
  • Actuator Realism: First-order motor lag ($\tau_m = 0.025\text{ s}$), thrust saturation, and Li-Po voltage discharge sag.
  • Perception Rendering: Real-time Unreal Engine 4 multi-camera socket streams (Overhead RGB-D, Onboard FPV, Chase).
  • Disturbance Modeling: Dryden wind turbulence up to $\sigma_g = 0.30\text{ m/s}$ and mass perturbation ($33\text{g} - 75\text{g}$).
